The quarter at a glance#

Between 1 July and 30 September 2026, RBNT moved out of Redbelly's reserved wallets and vesting contracts and into unnamed wallets, and more of it was staked. Staking grew by 18.2%, and holdings stayed concentrated in a small number of large wallets.

New to on-chain data? Five words you will see

Wallet
An address on Redbelly that can hold RBNT. One person can have many wallets, and one wallet (an exchange, say) can hold tokens for many people, so wallet counts are not people counts.
WRBNT
Wrapped RBNT: RBNT locked in a contract and handed back as a token that apps can use. Always backed 1 to 1, so we count each token once.
Staking
Locking RBNT in a Reddex pool to earn RBNT rewards. The no-lock pool allows withdrawals at any time. The 365-day pool locks each deposit for a year.
Snapshot
The chain's exact state at one block. This report compares the last block before 1 July with the last block of 30 September.
Holdings
For each wallet: plain RBNT + WRBNT + RBNT it has staked. Moving RBNT into staking does not lower what a wallet holds here.
What this report is, and is not. It describes what happened on the chain. It does not predict prices and it is not investment advice. Wallets are named only when they are already public: Redbelly's reserved wallets from the DAO treasury page, the Wrapped RBNT contract, the two Reddex staking pools, and contracts with a public name on Routescan. Every other wallet appears only inside a group.
01

Where RBNT sits#

Every RBNT on Redbelly sits in one of a handful of places. This is how much each held at the start and end of the quarter, and where the 139M that reached unnamed wallets came from.

In plain words

Most RBNT is still where it started: in wallets Redbelly reserved at launch (59.4% of all RBNT) and in contracts that release tokens over time (14.5%). During the quarter, vesting contracts released 73.9M RBNT, the reserved wallets sent out 43.8M on balance, and unnamed wallets ended up with 139.1M more. The total fell slightly (1.20M) because transaction fees leave the payer's wallet: 2.00M RBNT was paid in fees, and about 0.80M of that was paid back out to other addresses.

In plain words

RBNT in unnamed wallets is held very unevenly. The 15,321 unnamed wallets holding more than 10 RBNT are mostly small: 13,041 of them hold less than 10,000 RBNT each, and together they have 0.29% of the RBNT in this group. At the other end, 207 wallets with more than 1 million RBNT each hold 84.6%. A Gini score of 0.98 (where 1 would mean one wallet holds everything) says the same thing in one number. This is common for tokens whose large balances sit with exchanges, funds and early participants. Over the quarter the shape barely changed: the top 10 share moved +1.0 pts.

03

What the largest holders did#

Accumulation and selling among the biggest unnamed wallets, in groups. Holdings include staked RBNT, so moving tokens into a pool is not counted as selling.

We ranked every unnamed wallet by its holdings on 1 July, split the ranking into groups, and then looked at the same wallets on 30 September. Keeping the groups fixed shows what those holders actually did, rather than who happened to be at the top at the end. A wallet grew if its holdings rose by more than 1%, shrank if they fell by more than 1%, and otherwise stayed about the same.

In plain words

The 100 largest unnamed wallets on 1 July held 1,383.0M RBNT. By 30 September the same 100 wallets held 1,428.3M, and 550.8M of that was staked. 92 of them were still in the top 100 at the end of the quarter, and 8 new wallets joined. Shrinking wallets were a minority in every group. The data shows what changed, not why: a wallet can grow from buying, from a vesting release or from moving tokens between its owner's wallets.

Pool balances include reward funds as well as stakes. Both pools pay rewards in RBNT out of their own balance, so a pool's RBNT balance is people's stakes plus RBNT added to pay rewards. This report reads both the pool balance and the pool's own running total of stakes (totalStake()), and shows the gap as reward funds: 2.55M in the no-lock pool and 0.97M in the 365-day pool on 30 September. During the quarter 15M RBNT was added to the reward funds, and about 14.8M was paid out to stakers.
In plain words

More RBNT went into staking than came out: 196.4M was staked during the quarter and 80.9M was withdrawn, leaving 751.3M staked on 30 September (7.5% of all RBNT). The number of wallets with a stake went from 1,268 to 1,255, so the growth came from bigger stakes, not more stakers. The biggest single day was 28 Aug, when stakes rose by 73.7M (+72.3M in the no-lock pool); outside that day the total moved by less than 10.3M a day. The 365-day pool opened on 31 May 2025, so deposits from its first weeks began reaching the end of their one-year lock in mid-2026, and that is why some RBNT left it this quarter.

10 September 2026: three transfers in three minutes

The only movements of the multisig reserved wallets in the quarter, in time order. All three stayed between Redbelly's own wallets. TEAM (Locked), Reserve and USYD/CSIRO (Locked) did not change.

Source: balance change points found by binary search on each wallet's balance between blocks 3,025,809 and 3,209,414
Bridge. The RBNT Native OFT Adapter locks RBNT on Redbelly when it is bridged to another chain. It held 38.3M on 1 July and 43.2M on 30 September, a net increase of 4.9M: slightly more RBNT moved out to other chains than came back. RBNT on other chains is outside this report.
Exchanges. When the data was collected (26 Oct 2026), Routescan showed no public exchange labels for any Redbelly address. Exchange wallets therefore cannot be told apart in this edition, and any exchange balances sit inside the unnamed-wallets group. If labels appear, the next edition will report their flows separately.
In plain words

The large reserved-wallet moves this quarter were transfers between Redbelly's own wallets, all on 10 September. RBNT actually left the reserved wallets through Ecosystem (Unlocked), which tops up the staking reward funds and makes other ecosystem payments: 43.8M net over the quarter.

06

How this report was made#

Read directly from the chain, checked to the wei, and written down so the next edition can be produced the same way.

  1. Fix two snapshot blocks

    Block 3,025,809 is the last block before 1 July 2026 00:00 UTC and block 3,209,414 the last block of 30 September. Every balance is read at exactly these blocks from the public Redbelly RPC.

  2. Find every address

    Every block from the first to 26 Oct 2026 was read: 810,661 addresses that sent or received a transaction or were named inside one (for example in batch payouts and Redbelly's account registry), plus the WRBNT holder list from Routescan. 165,294 of them held RBNT, WRBNT or a stake at one of the two blocks.

  3. Count each token once

    Holdings = plain RBNT + WRBNT + RBNT staked in a Reddex pool. The RBNT inside the WRBNT contract is not counted again, and each pool's RBNT beyond its totalStake() is shown as reward funds.

  4. Group, never single out

    Publicly named wallets are named. Contracts are grouped by matching their code to verified contracts on Routescan. Every other wallet is reported only inside a group, and no unnamed address appears anywhere in the report or the data.

  5. Check that it adds up

    WRBNT balances add up to WRBNT's supply, stakers' balances add up to each pool's total, and holdings add up to the RBNT read: all to the wei, at both blocks.

Limits worth knowing

Wallets are not peopleOne person can hold many wallets, and an exchange wallet holds tokens for many people.
No exchange labels yetRoutescan labelled no Redbelly exchange wallets at collection time, so exchange balances sit inside the unnamed-wallets group.
Other chains are out of scopeOnly the RBNT locked in the bridge adapter on Redbelly is shown, not RBNT on other chains.
Contracts are not looked throughRBNT inside exchange pools, routers and other unnamed contracts is reported as one group.
